New to options? Start here

A short tour of the page, the key terms, and ideas for putting the data to work.

Call option
The right, not the obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price. It gains value when the market rises above the strike.
Put option
The right to sell the underlying at the strike price. It gains value when the market falls below the strike, which is why puts are used as protection.
Strike price
The fixed price written into the option. Each contract month lists many strikes above and below the current market.
Contract month and expiry
Options are grouped by the month they expire. The last trading day is shown next to each month in the chain; after it the contract settles and stops trading.
Premium
The price of the option, paid by the buyer to the seller.
Settlement price
The official end-of-day price TFEX sets for every series, including those that did not trade. Use it to compare days consistently.
Volume
How many contracts changed hands that day. High volume means the series was actively traded.
Open interest (OI)
How many contracts are still open at the end of the day. It rises when new positions are opened and falls when they are closed.
Underlying futures
In, at and out of the money
A call is in the money when the futures price is above its strike; a put when it is below. The strike closest to the futures price is at the money. The rest are out of the money and hold only time value.
Put/call ratio
Put open interest divided by call open interest. Above 1 means more puts are open than calls. Watch how it changes over time rather than reading one day alone.
Contract size

Where are positions concentrated?

The open interest chart in section 1 shows how many contracts are open at each strike. Strikes with very large open interest are levels many traders have a stake in, and they are often watched closely as expiry approaches.

Section 1 · pick the front month and the latest day

What is the market mood?

The put/call ratio in the summary and the open interest lines in section 3 show whether traders are adding puts (protection or bearish views) or calls (bullish views). A steady rise in the ratio says demand for protection is building.

Summary tiles · section 3

How fast does time value melt?

Choose a strike some distance from the futures price and look at its premium in section 2. With no move in the market, the premium shrinks day by day as expiry nears. The shape of that decline is time decay.

Section 2 · click an out-of-the-money row in the chain first

How did options react to a big move?

Find a day when the futures price jumped or fell, then use Prev and Next in section 1 to compare premiums and open interest before and after. The Chg columns show each series' change from the previous day.

Section 1 · Prev / Next buttons

Compare strikes on one day

Reading across one row of the chain shows the call and put at the same strike; reading down a column shows how premiums fall away from the money. This is the quickest way to see what protection costs at different levels.
